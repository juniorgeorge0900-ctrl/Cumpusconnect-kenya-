Earn Online as a Student: Legit Options and Scam Warnings

Online work is real work. It takes weeks of practice before most people land a first paying client, and nobody can promise you an income. This guide shows where to start and how to stay safe.

Online work students can start

  • Writing and editing: articles, blog posts, proofreading, CVs and cover letters.
  • Transcription: turning audio or video into text.
  • Virtual assistance and data entry: email, scheduling and spreadsheets for small businesses.
  • Graphic design and social media: posters, logos and page management for local shops.
  • Online tutoring: teach a subject you score well in.
  • Selling products: small e-commerce through social media, using real stock you can deliver.

Where to learn for free

Ajira Digital is a Government of Kenya programme that offers free training and mentorship in online work, including transcription, virtual assistance, digital marketing, design and e-commerce. Many universities and TVET colleges have an Ajira Club. Register at ajiradigital.go.ke or ask your university career office.

Where to find clients

Freelance marketplaces such as Upwork, Fiverr and Freelancer let you create a profile and apply for jobs. Competition is high and each platform takes a fee, so start with one skill and two or three good sample pieces.

Five steps to begin

  1. Pick one skill you can already do reasonably well.
  2. Take a free course, for example through Ajira Digital.
  3. Make two or three samples of your work.
  4. Create a clear profile with your samples and apply to a few jobs every day.
  5. Deliver on time, ask for a short review, then raise your prices slowly.

Scam warning signs

  • You are asked to pay a registration, training, uniform or "activation" fee to get work.
  • Someone promises guaranteed daily income or says you will double your money.
  • The offer comes only through WhatsApp or Telegram, from a personal email address, with no company website.
  • You are asked for your ID number, bank details, M-Pesa PIN or passwords.
  • You must recruit friends to earn, which is a pyramid scheme.
  • A client sends you more money than agreed and asks you to refund the difference.

Before you accept any offer, search the company name with the word "scam", ask for the terms in writing, and never pay to get a job. If you see a suspicious listing, tell us.
